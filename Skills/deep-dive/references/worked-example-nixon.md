# Worked example: the Nixon farewell session

This is the session the skill was distilled from, shown as method steps. Use it as an illustration of shape and standard, not as a template to copy. The subject here was one sentence from Nixon's farewell to his staff on August 9, 1974. The same method applies to any subject.

## Contents
- Phase 0 as it actually happened
- Phase 1 turns and what each did

- Phase 2 structure, mapped to the method

## Phase 0 as it actually happened

The user opened with the subject (the farewell line), the pull (a truism of life that people at the center of things cannot escape). The seen-list arrived later and mattered: the user had seen three Béla Tarr films but not the fourth, and said so only after a spoiler had slipped out. Lesson: ask for the seen-list at the start, because a boundary learned late costs trust.

## Phase 1 turns and what each did

1. **Opening depth request.** Gave the full line and its setting, split it into two claims (being hated is unavoidable; hatred only wins through your participation) and showed the second was partly false. Added Spinoza, Socrates, Marcus Aurelius and Nietzsche for the mechanism, the irony of the messenger, and the failure mode ("they're just haters"). Ended with a choice of angle.
2. **The user's reading of Nixon** (paranoid, thrown under the bus, wrestling with retribution, loved-then-hated, critics who only want to expose). Credited the accurate paranoia and the real wound, declined "well-adjusted" (self-aware but unchanged), and pushed on one thing: the critic's motive does not decide whether the claim is true. Reframed engage-versus-ignore as claim versus person.
3. **Secret Honor.** Answered "honest mirror or malarkey" with both: malarkey as history, sharp as psychology, because the invented conspiracy flatters him. The form (a monologue to nobody) was the real picture. Ended by asking which flattering story the user tells about their own case.
4. **The Underwood quote and the rain line.** Credited the Sátántangó comparison, then separated explanation from license (the cold happens to you, the enemies list is what you do with the fever). Treated Underwood as Nixon with the bill removed. Ended by asking what the hatred is for.
5. **Three points on leadership and betrayal.** Gave each a truth and a self-protective move. Noted that silence is not a stab. Ended by asking whether anyone had actually acted against them.
6. **"There's a lot I'm not telling"** plus the request for the full integrated analysis, to be read aloud, with no length limit. That triggered Phase 2.

## The ledger at synthesis time

- Claims: Nixon was paranoid but not unjustifiably so; retribution toward his father; resentment from the Kennedy loss; reflection happens when we fall; hatred and resentment as a tango (the Sátántangó analogy).
- Borrowed lines: Underwood's speech on hate; Wendy Rhoades on academia; "left in the rain and not catching a cold".
- Pushed on: "well-adjusted"; the father link (thin evidence); critic's motive; silence as stab.
- Stepped back: none explicit, but he moved from "paranoid president thrown under the bus" toward the partial defense plus the guilt.
- Withheld: "a lot I'm not telling". Honored by not guessing.

## Phase 2 structure, mapped to the method

1. Opening: acknowledged the withheld material and owned the earlier spoiler.
2. Object: the speech itself, the night-before television address against the unscripted morning, the tape released four days earlier, the Roosevelt reading.
3. Claims: the two halves of the line.
4. Background: Whittier, the grocery store, two brothers lost, the Harvard scholarship he could not use, the firms that turned him away, Kennedy, the 1962 press conference. Adjusted the father reading: reasonable, undocumented.
5. Steelman both ways: precedent in other administrations and selective enforcement against his own cover-up; the 1972 landslide showing the compulsion no longer served strategy. "Well-adjusted" revisited as self-aware but undigested. Later years: rehabilitation and the funeral eulogy.
6. The user's readings: Secret Honor as defense mechanism; the Underwood and rain points; each tied back by name.
7. Lenses, in this order: Aristotle (the pleasure of the list), Achilles (wrath ends through recognition), the Oresteia (the Furies given a forum), Philoctetes (abandonment of the inconvenient), Timon (indiscriminate love and hate), Thucydides (factional vocabulary, with Sayre's law), Tarr's three films (resentment organized, the man who becomes the dog, weather that nobody sent), Spinoza (the cause-idea), Kohlhaas (the counter-lens), Girard (the scapegoat and the critics who want you worse than you thought), Arendt (forgiveness needs others).
8. The chain: wound, story, culprit, list, instrument, closed room, with an exit at each.
9. Back to the user: the line as a test to run on oneself, two columns, a steelman exercise for the justified criticism, and a caution about borrowing the subject's stature.
10. Close: a flat final sentence on the test.

## What made it land

- Every lens made a claim about the mechanism and was set against a break point.
- The counter-lens (Kohlhaas) and the Tarr correction kept the piece from being agreement.
- The user's phrases (the rain, the stab, the tango) returned as anchors, credited by name.
- Where the evidence was thin the piece said so in a clause and moved on.
- The prose obeyed the ear: no formatting, short spoken signposts, one quote at most per source.
- The close stopped at the last point.

## Transfer sketch

For a different subject, say a physicist's line about what his work had done, the same steps apply. Phase 0 fixes the exact sentence and what the user has read. Split the line into claims and test each. Gather the setting: where and when it was said and what had just happened. Build a chain for the process underneath it (responsibility, perhaps, or complicity). Choose lenses by what each says about that process, include one that argues against the user's thesis, and finish with the stage-by-stage exits and a flat final sentence.
