# Writing for the ear

Use these rules when the user will hear the piece through a text-to-speech voice or read it aloud, which is the default for Phase 2. They exist because a listener cannot scan back, skip ahead, or see structure, so the prose has to carry the structure itself.

If the user has their own writing rules (an anti-AI-writing skill, a style guide), load them first. Where they conflict with anything here, theirs win.

## Form
- Plain paragraphs only. No headers, bullets, bold, tables, or markdown of any kind. A text-to-speech voice reads symbols aloud or drops them.
- Paragraphs of roughly 80 to 160 words, each carrying one move.
- Spoken signposts instead of headings: "The speech first." "Now the Greek side." "Now to you." A signpost is one short sentence at the start of a paragraph.
- Open each lens paragraph by naming the lens and its claim, so the listener knows why it has arrived.
- Give a one-sentence orientation before the chain ("So here is the integrated version"), since listeners need a cue that the synthesis is starting.

## Sentences
- Alternate short sentences (three to eight words) with longer ones (fifteen to twenty-five). Uniform rhythm lulls a listener.
- After a long gap, repeat the noun in place of a pronoun, so the listener does not lose the referent.
- State bare facts directly. Do not assert that something is significant; show what it changes.
- Use first person where the stance is Claude's own ("I'd call that a reasonable reading").

## Things a voice reads badly
- No em dashes, parentheses, slashes, semicolon chains, or abbreviations such as "e.g." and "i.e."
- Write years and dates as a voice would say them. Spell out symbols and units.
- Gloss unfamiliar foreign terms once, in a clause, or avoid them. Keep titles and names consistent so the voice pronounces them the same way each time.
- Avoid long lists. A voice cannot make a list scannable. Two items or four, in a sentence, not three by reflex.

## Patterns to avoid
- Negative-parallelism pivots ("it is not X, it is Y"). State the Y.
- Rule-of-three flourishes (three adjectives, three clauses) used to fake depth.
- Significance padding ("a testament to", "reflecting its enduring") and present-participle tails ("..., marking a turning point").
- Cycling synonyms for one subject. Use the name, then "he", "she" or "it".
- Wrap-up closers: "in conclusion", "overall", a moral, a hopeful forecast, a closing question. End on the last point.
- Stock vocabulary: delve, tapestry, landscape (figurative), pivotal, crucial, profound, testament, resonate, underscore.

## Quotations
- Under fifteen words, one per source, ideally one in the whole piece per work. Paraphrase the rest.
- Never reproduce lyrics, poems, or long passages from books, articles or scripts.

## Length checks
- About 150 words per minute spoken. 3,500 words is roughly 25 minutes.
- If the piece will exceed about 7,000 words, split it into two parts and say so at the start of part one.
